# Writing the owner reads

The owner decides from words alone, with neither the code nor the build's records open. This rule covers every text whose reader is the owner: the wording of the hand-over page, the record fields that page prints as written (`.claude/skills/implement-spec/SKILL.md` names them), a pull request's title and description, and what you say to the owner in chat (a status update, an answer, a closing message). Whatever a program or another agent reads keeps its own conventions: field names and the values a program matches on (ids, `fate`, `severity`, `finder`, `pass`, paths, commits), file formats, code, test names, logs, what a script prints to the terminal, commit messages, and messages between agents in either direction. A subagent's final message is one of those: its reader is the agent that started it.

**The test,** for a page or a message as a whole: after its first paragraph the owner has the answer or knows what happened and, where there is something to say, why it matters to them and whether they have to do anything. A single field or line (a `what`, a `why`, one line of `hand-over.json`) says its one thing in full; the page puts them in that order.

- **Outcome first, source last.** Lead with the conclusion as a whole sentence with a subject ("The build stopped at ticket 04"). Follow it with why it matters and what the owner should do, when there is something to say. Several conclusions go in the order the owner must act on them. Where a conclusion is recorded (file, section, id, commit, ticket number) comes after the conclusion.
- **Shown before explained.** On the page, one sentence of conclusion is followed by what is drawn: the status tiles, the counts and the list of what the owner can do. Each item on that list shows what it is and what to do about it; why it matters, what the page's words mean and where a number comes from open under a fold beside it. Paragraphs stacked above the first tile are a fault, however clear each sentence is. In chat, the same: the answer, then a short list, then the detail.
- **Sentences that say who did what:** "The build left it unfixed." A heading, a label or a table cell may stay a phrase.
- **Once, for the group.** On a page or in a message, when several items share a status or a reason, state it once above them; each item then carries only what is its own. A finding's line and a fate's line stay complete, because the page groups those; it does not group the lines of `hand-over.json`.
- **The owner's words.** Describe what a user sees and what the owner can do. A word from the build process (fate, finder, pass, walk guard) or from the code (a function, a file) is replaced by what it means, or follows the sentence it supports.
- **A claim keeps the strength of its evidence.** Where the owner could take a statement for more than it is, say which it is: something run ("I ran the checks on the last commit and they passed"), something looked up ("the test's file is in the last commit"), a record's claim ("the record marks it as fixed"), an assumption ("I assume the old log merged them; nothing records it"), or advice ("I suggest trying the export first"). Say plainly what is unknown or was not checked. State a severity, an impact, a verification or a certainty only when you established it yourself or a record gives it.
- **Evidence stays.** Clearer is the aim, and shorter is not. A rewrite drops no id, commit, test or quoted wording: each stays reachable from the conclusion it supports, in the same text or in the record the page shows under it.
